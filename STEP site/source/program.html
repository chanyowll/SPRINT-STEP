Program brief

What STEP is for

The Science and Technology Entrepreneurship Program (STEP) equips scientists and engineers with the entrepreneurial skills to create sustainable business models, prepare successful FASTRAC grant proposals, catalyse the establishment of spin-off companies that commercialize their technologies, and foster a culture of innovation and entrepreneurship that contributes to Philippine economic development.

STEP is the second phase of SPRINT — the Short-term Program for Researchers on Innovation and Technopreneurship — and follows the Filipinnovation Entrepreneurship Corps (FEC). It is funded by DOST-PCIEERD and implemented by the Ateneo Intellectual Property Office.

01

Entrepreneurial skills

Train researchers to apply what they learned in FEC and build sustainable business models in a short, focused time frame.

02

FASTRAC proposals

Guide each team through the preparation of a DOST FASTRAC grant proposal.

03

Spin-off companies

Foster the creation of spin-offs that commercialize the innovations developed by scientists and engineers.

04

Innovation culture

Promote innovation and entrepreneurship in the Philippines and contribute to economic development.

Training setups

Five ways teams learn

Online · weekly

Online learning sessions

One three-hour Tuesday morning over Zoom, one topic a week, taught by faculty from the Ateneo School of Management and invited specialists. Lecture, discussion, and a worked exercise on the team's own technology — you leave with the week's work already started.

  • WhenTuesdays, 9:00 AM–12:00 NN
  • FormatZoom, recorded — watch it later if you cannot join
  • After eachOne team output, uploaded by Friday
Per team · weekly

Mentoring sessions

Every team is paired with a mentor drawn from business practitioners and faculty. An hour and a half a week to work through the assignment, prepare for Saturday, and decide what to test next.

  • WhenAny slot the team picks, Wednesday to Friday
  • Length1 hour, online
  • MentorsOne assigned mentor for the whole cycle
Saturdays · panels A, B and C

Feedback sessions

Teams play a five-minute video of the week's output to a panel of three entrepreneurs and faculty, then take twenty-five minutes of questions. The comments become the team's short list for the week ahead — nothing to write up afterwards.

  • Format5-minute video, then 25 minutes of Q&A
  • Your slot30 minutes — you do not sit through the whole morning
  • Panel3 panelists per session, rotating
On site · launch and checkpoint

On-site events

Everyone gathers in person twice before Demo Day. The Program Launch opens the cycle with team formation, an overview of technology entrepreneurship, pathway ideation and the mentor introduction. Halfway through, the Mid-Program Checkpoint brings a technology review, mentoring, pitch refinement and a business model validation workshop.

  • Program LaunchWeek 1 · October 29–30, 2026
  • Mid-Program CheckpointWeek 6 · December 3–4, 2026
On site · closing

Demo Day and graduation

The cycle closes on site. On Demo Day teams give their final presentations, with their technology roadmap, and submit the FASTRAC capsule. The next day is the graduation ceremony and ecosystem networking.

  • Demo DayWeek 12 · Thursday, January 28, 2027
  • GraduationWeek 12 · Friday, January 29, 2027
  • AlsoTechnology roadmap, FASTRAC capsule submission

Curriculum

The weekly topics

One topic per week. Open a week to see what teams produce.

Assessment

How teams are graded

30% Team output
30% FASTRAC proposal
20% Demo Day
20% Attendance

Team output

Weekly deliverables, panel feedback scores and mentor reports.

FASTRAC proposal

Technical gaps closed, market and business model validated, spin-off simulation.

Demo Day

Market size, customer segmentation, evidence, and how well the pitch lands.

Attendance

At least three team members present at every learning and feedback session.

How teams are selected

Teams come from DOST-PCIEERD-funded projects, chosen on their Filipinnovation Entrepreneurship Corps performance, the market potential of their technology, and their commitment to the four hours a week the programme asks for.
